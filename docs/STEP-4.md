## Question

On identical simulated timetable scenarios, does a permutation Genetic Algorithm improve session coverage or soft time-preference satisfaction compared with deterministic first-fit, while keeping hard room, faculty and cohort conflicts at zero?

## Data and scenarios

- Source: `data/processed/cleaned_scenarios.csv`, generated by the project's Step 2 scenario pipeline.
- The source contains 10,000 simulated scenarios: normal, boundary and stress cases. It is not real university scheduling data.
- Evaluation sample: 300 scenarios, selected reproducibly as up to 100 from each category (normal, boundary and stress), using `random_state=42`.
- The exact same scenario IDs and meeting requests are run by both methods. Selected scenario IDs and per-method measures are recorded in `data/reports/step4_paired_scenario_results.csv`.

## Baseline rule

First-fit processes courses in course-code order and expands each course's `weekly_sessions`. For every meeting it scans candidate start slots from S1 onward and then compatible rooms (R1/R2/R3 order). It accepts the first assignment that avoids room, faculty and cohort overlap. If no assignment is possible, the meeting is marked unplaced. A meeting that starts outside its course's `slot_id` preference receives one soft preference penalty.

## Genetic Algorithm rule

The GA chromosome is a permutation of individual requested meetings. Its decoder places meetings in chromosome order, trying compatible start slots nearest to the course's preferred `slot_id`, then rooms. The decoder rejects conflicting placements, so hard room/faculty/cohort conflicts are never exchanged for a better score. The fitness is `1000 × unplaced sessions + preference penalties`, so placing an additional meeting takes priority over reducing a soft penalty. Order crossover and swap mutation search alternative meeting orders. Settings: population 12, generations 10, fixed deterministic seeds per scenario.

### Interpretation

The GA's mean coverage was 3.62 percentage points higher than first-fit. The GA left 0.60 fewer sessions unplaced per scenario on average. The GA recorded 510 fewer soft preference penalties over the evaluated scenarios.

A hard-violation total other than zero would indicate an invalid schedule and must be investigated, even if coverage or preference scores are better. Room-slot utilization should be interpreted alongside coverage and unplaced meetings, not as a stand-alone success measure.

## Limitations and responsible use

The data is simulated from simplified assumptions: three example rooms, ten available slots and five courses per scenario. The evaluation uses a fixed sample of up to 100 scenarios per category to keep repeatable GA runs practical; it is not evidence of real-world university performance. The GA depends on its encoded representation, fitness priorities, population and generation settings; it is not guaranteed to find a global optimum. A zero clash count does not mean every request was scheduled, so unplaced sessions are reported separately.
