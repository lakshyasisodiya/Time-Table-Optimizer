## Question

On identical simulated timetable scenarios, does a permutation Genetic Algorithm improve session coverage or soft time-preference satisfaction compared with deterministic first-fit, while keeping hard room, faculty and cohort conflicts at zero?

## Data and scenarios

- Source: `data/processed/cleaned_scenarios.csv`, generated by the project's Step 2 scenario pipeline.
- The source contains 10,000 simulated scenarios: normal, boundary and stress cases. It is not real university scheduling data.
- Evaluation set: all 10,000 scenarios from Step 2; no scenario sampling is performed.
- Scenario ID check: every ID has exactly five distinct course rows and belongs to exactly one category (normal, boundary, or stress). This catches IDs accidentally shared across scenario groups.
- The exact same scenario IDs and meeting requests are run by both methods. Scenario IDs and per-method measures are recorded in `data/reports/step4_paired_scenario_results.csv`.

## Baseline rule

First-fit processes courses in course-code order and expands each course's `weekly_sessions`. For every meeting it scans candidate start slots from S1 onward and then compatible rooms (R1/R2/R3 order). It accepts the first assignment that avoids room, faculty and cohort overlap. If no assignment is possible, the meeting is marked unplaced. A meeting that starts outside its course's `slot_id` preference receives one soft preference penalty.

## Genetic Algorithm rule

The GA chromosome is a permutation of individual requested meetings. Its decoder places meetings in chromosome order, trying compatible start slots nearest to the course's preferred `slot_id`, then rooms. The decoder rejects conflicting placements, so hard room/faculty/cohort conflicts are never exchanged for a better score. The fitness is `1000 × unplaced sessions + preference penalties`, so placing an additional meeting takes priority over reducing a soft penalty. Order crossover and swap mutation search alternative meeting orders. Settings: population 8, generations 5, fixed deterministic seeds per scenario.

### Interpretation

Across all scenarios, the GA scheduled 4,536 more sessions than first-fit (89,381 versus 84,845). The GA's mean coverage was 3.12 percentage points higher than first-fit. The GA left 0.46 fewer sessions unplaced per scenario on average. The GA recorded 20,989 fewer soft preference penalties over the evaluated scenarios. Both methods recorded zero hard violations across the complete benchmark.

The GA improves placement and soft preference penalties on this simulated benchmark, but it still leaves sessions unplaced. Those requests are not necessarily mathematically infeasible; they may remain unplaced because of the finite GA search budget and the decoder's greedy placement choices. Room-slot utilization should be interpreted alongside coverage and unplaced meetings, not as a stand-alone success measure.

## Limitations and responsible use

The data is simulated from simplified assumptions: three example rooms, ten available slots and five courses per scenario. The comparison uses all 10,000 generated scenarios, but this is still not evidence of performance on a real university timetable. The GA uses a population of 8 and 5 generations per scenario. It depends on its encoded representation, fitness priorities, population and generation settings; it is not guaranteed to find a global optimum. Unplaced sessions are not necessarily truly infeasible: the greedy decoder or finite GA search may fail to find another valid ordering. A zero clash count does not mean every request was scheduled, so unplaced sessions are reported separately.
